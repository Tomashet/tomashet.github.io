Research Profile

PhD candidate at McMaster University researching reinforcement learning and safety for autonomous systems operating under nonstationarity. Work spans safe and continual reinforcement learning, adaptive safety constraints, proactive context forecasting, runtime assurance, and evaluation under distribution shift — with the long-term goal of making adaptation itself a subject of safety assurance. Earlier work in automotive functional safety grounds this research in a systems-safety approach to learning-enabled and autonomous systems.

Core expertise: Safe RL · Continual / Adaptive Learning · Nonstationarity & Distribution Shift · Runtime Assurance & Shielding · Sequential Decision-Making · Autonomous Systems · Functional Safety

Research Experience

PhD Researcher — AI Safety & Reinforcement Learning

2022 – Present

McMaster University, Department of Computing and Software · Hamilton, ON

  • Conduct an independent research program on maintaining safety as environmental conditions, learned knowledge, and safety constraints evolve over time.
  • Designed context-dependent and adaptation-speed safety constraints for nonstationary RL, including mechanisms for detecting when environmental change exceeds an agent's safe adaptation capacity.
  • Developed proactive, context-forecasted safety constraints that infer latent context, forecast its evolution, quantify predictive uncertainty, and construct anticipatory constraints before unsafe behavior emerges.
  • Built CPSS (Constraint Projection Safety Shield), a runtime mechanism converting cumulative safety budgets into enforceable, action-level constraints; reduced average collision rate by 91.6% (0.082 → 0.007) versus an unshielded DQN baseline across four highway-env driving tasks.
  • Architected LILAC+, a modular framework integrating context-based, adaptation-speed, and soft-to-hard safety constraints; reduced safety violations relative to unconstrained and fixed-constraint baselines across stationary, seen-nonstationary, and unseen-nonstationary regimes while maintaining competitive reward.
  • Designed and ran RL experiments (DQN/PPO) in nonstationary driving environments; CoLLAs 2026 evaluation achieved an 87.2% collision-rate reduction on the main benchmark and 92–97% reduction across three held-out road layouts.
  • Developing SafeShiftRL, a modular benchmark for evaluating safety under stationary, seen-nonstationary, and unseen-nonstationary conditions.
