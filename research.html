Research Focus

My research focuses on AI safety for adaptive and autonomous systems, with particular interests in safe and continual reinforcement learning, safety under nonstationarity, runtime safety, and functional safety for safety-critical systems.

How can an autonomous learning system remain safe when its environment, knowledge, operating conditions, and the concrete constraints required for safe behavior change over time?

My long-term goal is to develop AI systems for which safety is not merely a static property specified at design time, but a capability maintained throughout operation. Such systems should recognize safety-relevant changes, anticipate emerging risks, adapt their safety mechanisms when appropriate, and act conservatively when reliable adaptation is not possible.

Current Research: Adaptive Safety under Nonstationarity

My current research focuses on safe continual reinforcement learning in nonstationary environments. Many safe-RL formulations assume that safety constraints or models specified for a particular operating regime remain valid. Under nonstationarity, however, a previously appropriate constraint can become obsolete, overly conservative, or unsafe. Moreover, even when an appropriate new constraint can be identified, the environment may change faster than the learning system can safely adapt. These two problems—constraint validity and adaptation speed—are central to my research.

I address them by treating concrete safety constraints as adaptive objects anchored to more stable, higher-level safety goals. My LILAC+ framework combines context-based constraints that adapt safety requirements to current and predicted conditions; adaptation-speed constraints that assess whether required adaptation exceeds the agent's estimated capacity; and soft-to-hard mechanisms that translate cumulative safety budgets into local constraints suitable for runtime enforcement.

A related line of my work develops proactive context-forecasted safety constraints. Rather than waiting until a distribution shift or safety violation occurs, the system infers environmental context, forecasts its evolution, accounts for predictive uncertainty, and constructs anticipatory safety constraints. The objective is to move safety from reactive correction toward proactive risk avoidance.

I also develop evaluation methodologies for safe continual RL under controlled nonstationarity, including stationary, seen-nonstationary, and unseen-nonstationary regimes, with explicit evaluation of constraint validity, physical safety, intervention burden, and adaptation behavior.

Research Directions

My current work motivates several connected directions toward a broader methodology for adaptive AI safety.

01

Predictive and Proactive AI Safety

I am interested in systems that reason not only about current risk but also about plausible future contexts and the future validity of their own safety assumptions. This includes context and risk forecasting, uncertainty calibration, and anticipatory constraint construction. A central question is how AI systems can anticipate the safety consequences of developing changes before unsafe conditions arise.

02

Adaptive Runtime Safety and AI Control

I am interested in architectures combining policy-level adaptation with runtime monitoring, shielding, dynamic constraints, fallback behavior, and adaptive intervention. When environmental change exceeds an agent's ability to adapt safely, the system may need to tighten constraints, restrict autonomy, invoke fallback mechanisms, seek external supervision, and determine when normal autonomous operation can safely resume.

03

Safety under Novelty and Unmodelled Change

A particularly difficult problem arises when an autonomous system encounters conditions outside its learned experience. I am interested in detecting unfamiliar contexts, quantifying epistemic uncertainty, determining when learned predictions or constraints should no longer be trusted, and connecting these assessments to conservative runtime behavior.

How should an autonomous system recognize the limits of its own safety model?
04

Long-Horizon Agent Safety

As AI systems become increasingly autonomous, safety must account for accumulated risk and evolving interactions over trajectories, rather than individual actions alone. I am interested in trajectory-level risk monitoring, dynamic safety budgets, and adaptive intervention policies that coordinate local safety decisions with longer-term safety requirements.

05

Multi-Agent Safety under Nonstationarity

Multiple learning agents create an important source of endogenous nonstationarity because their behavior and strategies can themselves change. I am interested in forecasting safety-relevant changes in other agents, adapting constraints to changing interaction regimes, detecting emerging collective risks, and extending adaptive-safety methodologies to multi-agent environments.

From Functional Safety to Adaptive AI Safety

My AI-safety research grows from a broader background in functional safety and safety-critical software-intensive systems, including autonomous and automotive systems. Classical functional-safety engineering contributes concepts such as specification, architectural assurance, runtime monitoring, fault handling, and safe fallback.

Learning-enabled systems introduce an additional challenge: system behavior and the conditions relevant to safety can continue changing after deployment. I am interested in bringing these perspectives together to develop safety-engineering principles for adaptive systems whose behavior and operating conditions cannot be completely specified or validated in advance.

Research Vision

My broader objective is to contribute to a science and engineering methodology for adaptive AI safety. Reinforcement learning provides a useful setting because learning, sequential decision-making, environmental change, and safety interact explicitly, but the underlying problem extends to increasingly capable autonomous and agentic AI systems operating over long periods in changing environments.

I aim to develop theory, algorithms, runtime mechanisms, and evaluation methodologies that connect adaptive learning with dependable safety assurance. The long-term goal is AI that can recognize when its assumptions are changing, anticipate safety-relevant consequences, adapt its safety mechanisms when justified, and remain conservative when reliable adaptation cannot be assured.

Ultimately, I am interested in making adaptation itself a subject of safety assurance.

My goal is to develop methods and principles that make adaptation itself a subject of safety assurance — enabling intelligent systems to remain safe as the world they operate in continues to change.
